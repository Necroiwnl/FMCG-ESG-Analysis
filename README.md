Project Objective

This project analyzes the official sustainability reports (BRSR) of India's top 5 FMCG companies to evaluate their plastic packaging performance. The goal was to move beyond stated claims and use data to identify leaders in efficiency, circularity, and, most importantly, reporting transparency.

---

Process

1.  **Data Collection:** Manually sourced and extracted financial and environmental data from the latest official Annual Reports of HUL, ITC, Nestlé, Dabur, and Britannia.
2.  **Data Analysis & KPI Development:** Cleaned the data and developed two custom KPIs to measure performance:
    * **Plastic Intensity (g / ₹ Cr):** To measure packaging efficiency.
    * **Circularity Gap (%):** To measure the credibility of "plastic positive" claims.
3.  **Data Visualization:** Designed and built a one-page interactive dashboard in Power BI to present the findings in a clear and compelling narrative.

---

Key Insights

> **Insight 1: The Efficiency Divide**
> Nestlé demonstrates superior packaging efficiency, using over 50% less plastic per crore of revenue than its competitor, Britannia. This highlights a significant operational advantage.

> **Insight 2: Strong "Plastic Positive" Performance**
> Both HUL and Nestlé are significantly "plastic positive," collecting and processing far more plastic waste than they generate. Nestlé is a clear outlier, managing nearly 26 times its own plastic footprint.

> **Insight 3: The Transparency Risk**
> A critical finding is the transparency divide. While leaders like HUL and Nestlé provide clear data, other major companies like ITC and Dabur do not. This lack of disclosure presents a major risk for investors assessing their true environmental impact.
